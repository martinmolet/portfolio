Teaching Case study 02 / 03

ECVAix-en-Provence

Two workshops with Master 1 students on designing and documenting a Design System. Not theory alone: hands-on work and solid foundations for real projects.

01
Fundamentals

Giving the right mental models before touching Figma.

Before building anything, you need to understand why an atomic hierarchy holds up in real work. Atoms, molecules, organisms: not a textbook taxonomy, but a way to think about composition and maintenance.

Variables and tokens come next: the difference between primitives and semantics, how a well-named token documents its own usage. And theming as a quality test for the system: a DS that can’t be themed has no real contract.

A DS that can’t be themed is a DS without a real contract.
02
Library

Each student builds their own Figma library from A to Z.

Not a file to fill in. A complete library, starting from primitive colors and ending with templates. The exercise forces real choices: how to name a token, where a component’s boundary sits, what to document.

  • 01Define a palette and name tokens semantically
  • 02Build the atoms: buttons, inputs, labels
  • 03Compose molecules: form fields, list items
  • 04Assemble organisms: forms, navigation, cards
  • 05Document every component: usage, rules, constraints

Short one-on-one reviews help unblock, validate naming choices and point out the classic traps: a component too broad, a token too specific, docs too technical.

03
Modularity

Breaking down an existing component to understand what should be a token, a slot, a prop.

We take an existing complex component: a product card, a multi-step modal. We take it apart. We identify what should stay fixed, what should be flexible, what should be driven by tokens.

A component that’s too clever becomes impossible to maintain. Modularity pays off in the long run.

Each student leaves with a refactored version and a note justifying their breakdown choices. Not a correction: a conversation about trade-offs.

04
Documentation

Documenting what makes a component usable by someone who wasn’t in the room.

Documentation isn’t a formality. It’s what turns a component into a shareable tool. Without docs, a DS is a private museum only its author can navigate.

  • 01Usage: when to use it, when to avoid it
  • 02Best practices with valid and invalid visual examples
  • 03Consistency rules: alignment, spacing, content
  • 04Variants: valid combinations only
  • 05Tokens used: to enable theming
05
Teaching

Exchange and practice. Never more than 10 minutes of static slides.

5 days per cohort, alternating short presentations, live demos and immediate practice. Questions are encouraged, counter-examples valued. Students bring their own projects as live case studies.

The goal isn’t to leave with textbook basics: it’s to leave with applicable ones. Foundations that hold up in a real project, with real trade-offs and real deadlines.
